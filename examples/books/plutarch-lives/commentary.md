Plutarch, a Greek writer living under Roman rule around 100 AD, wrote these lives in pairs: a Greek and a Roman whose characters or fortunes seemed alike, usually followed by a short comparison of the two.

He says plainly that he is writing lives, not histories. He cares less about battles and dates than about character: a passing remark or a small habit, he thought, can show more of a person than the sieges they won. Read him for the people, and treat his facts with care, since he often records more than one version of a story and lets the reader choose.

This is the Dryden translation as revised by Arthur Hugh Clough and published in 1859, so the English is Victorian, and the names are often the Latin forms (Hercules, Jupiter, Neptune) rather than the Greek ones (Heracles, Zeus, Poseidon).
